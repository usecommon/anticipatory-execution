# AI adjudication report

I reviewed all 62 selected records, including the 20 preselected controls, and explicitly authored each decision in `judge_decisions.txt` before formatting and validating `adjudication.jsonl` with Python's standard library. Results are 23 reusable, 13 not reusable, and 26 unresolved. Selection combined binary disagreement, any initial ambiguity flag, and controls; these counts describe that selected set, not the full 200-item prevalence.

The judgments were based on the candidate work and frozen rubric rather than majority voting. Agreement did not settle unspecified-output cases. In particular, a generic message draft, function draft, or topic-free search can denote substantially different work products. Retaining a heading or a possible relevant result is not enough evidence to decide whether material re-execution is avoided. The unresolved decisions preserve this evidential gap rather than treating initial unanimous labels as gold labels.

The four binary disagreements have the following outcomes:

- T026-P3 is reusable: vendor-claim collection can supply the claims to check for independent reproduction. The later source rule controls final inclusion and does not prohibit collecting those claims. My initial zero conflated collection with inclusion.
- T037-P2 remains unresolved: a pricing section could be a heading with TBD or substantive pricing that must be removed. Initial raters made different assumptions about the absent output.
- T048-P1 remains unresolved: the unspecified morning schedule might require either a minor insertion or extensive rescheduling around appointment and travel.
- T050-P1 remains unresolved: neither the existing ranking nor the materiality threshold for moving the client task is specified.

Other consequential findings concern preference and conditional action. A staged Monday invitation is not equivalent to committing a disallowed date: Tuesday is only preferred, and availability is unknown. A staged retry patch is not itself the prohibited premature settings change, while the unknown cause determines eventual reuse. Both remain unresolved. By contrast, an unconditional thermostat commit explicitly bypasses its required gate and is not reusable; checking temperature or the door sensor evaluates a required condition and is reusable. A long-runtime-only filter must be changed to evaluate the user's shorter-runtime preference, even if long documentaries are possible fallbacks.

The main rubric questions are how to treat unspecified outputs; how much surviving structure counts as nonmaterial revision; whether unsupported filtering should be judged as a changed rule or by surviving candidate records; and how to distinguish a conditional reusable preparation from a premature commit. The broad-retrieval worked example supports filtering a relevant topical pool. It does not clearly establish whether entirely unspecified location, subject, or candidate coverage should be presumed present. I applied that example to specified topical work and left cases with insufficient coverage evidence unresolved. Exact versus approximate capacity and inclusive versus strict runtime boundaries were treated as minor filtering refinements. These are adjudication interpretations, not retroactive changes to the frozen rubric.
