# Annotator A methods

These are AI-generated judgments by an independent blind AI annotator, not human ratings. The only task input files read were `A/RUBRIC.txt` and `A/items.jsonl`. No repository context, other annotator submissions, hidden mappings, owner key, external sources, or candidate-action results were consulted. No candidate actions were executed, and no browsing or delegation was used.

I considered each record's prefix, candidate action, arguments, and execution class before its full instruction, then explicitly authored its binary judgment, fractional survival estimate, confidence, ambiguity flag, and individual rationale. I judged work-product reuse rather than probable user intent. Broad retrieval was assessed for whether surviving results could be filtered; drafts were assessed for surviving structure and substantive content; committed actions were assessed against their stated conditions. Underspecified outputs and soft preferences received uncertainty flags where they affected the reuse judgment. No keyword classifier, default-label assignment, or order-based label inference was used.

`author_labels.py` contains all 200 individually authored judgments. Python standard-library code formats them into `submission.jsonl` and validates record count, unique IDs, exact coverage against the assigned input, allowed numeric ranges, flag values, and nonempty rationales. The code does not infer or generate judgments from item text.

Procedural details and limitations: the first combined display of rubric and items was truncated, so the obscured middle records were read in bounded follow-up displays before labeling. Records were displayed in batches rather than through an interface that mechanically conceals the full instruction; prefix/action-before-full reading was followed cognitively within each record. Fractional utility estimates are subjective, since actual candidate work products are absent. Broad searches and generic drafts therefore require assumptions about what survives, recorded in rationales and ambiguity flags. These ratings do not establish human reliability, and no agreement statistic or G1 pass/fail claim is made here.
